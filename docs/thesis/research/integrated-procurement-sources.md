## Evidence guide

| Source | Evidence type | Appropriate use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chan and Owusu (2022) | Structured literature review | Explain procurement activities and adoption concepts. |
| Alabdali and Salam (2022) | Survey and structural equation modelling | Discuss statistical relationships between digital transformation, procurement, and competitive advantage. |
| Sivasubramaniam et al. (2024) | Qualitative expert interviews | Discuss perceived benefits, tradeoffs, and conditions of centralization. |
| Gurgun et al. (2024) | Practitioner survey, barrier ranking, and interviews | Explain operational barriers to construction procurement integration. |
| Parisi and Donyavi (2026) | Reported operational case-study outcomes; abstract verified | Attribute the reported processing-time reduction to the studied SMEs. |
| Bacarisas et al. (2026) | Survey perceptions and correlation; abstract verified | Discuss Philippine digital procurement experiences and integration gaps. |

These evidence types should retain their qualifications in the manuscript. A review is not a deployment evaluation; expert views are not measured savings; survey associations do not establish causation. None of the sources establishes ConstructSync's effectiveness at HML Technologies and Services Inc.

## 1. Chan and Owusu (2022)

**APA 7:** Chan, A. P. C., & Owusu, E. K. (2022). Evolution of electronic procurement: Contemporary review of adoption and implementation strategies. *Buildings, 12*(2), Article 198. https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings12020198

**Publication and verification:** Published 9 February 2022. Metadata, abstract, methods, and relevant discussion were verified through indexed original [MDPI article text](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/12/2/198).

**Method and findings:** A structured review selected 64 manuscripts after screening and used bibliometric, scientometric, thematic, and content analyses. The review maps the evolution of electronic procurement and identifies activities including electronic sourcing, tendering, invoicing, auctions, and reporting. It develops a conceptual adoption framework and treats adoption as an ongoing process requiring readiness.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports presenting procurement as connected activities extending beyond an isolated purchase transaction. The authors explicitly report no empirical survey or deployment assessment; the framework still requires contextual validation. Do not attribute processing-time or cost reductions to this review.

## 2. Alabdali and Salam (2022)

**APA 7:** Alabdali, M. A., & Salam, M. A. (2022). The impact of digital transformation on supply chain procurement for creating competitive advantage: An empirical study. *Sustainability, 14*(19), Article 12269. https://doi.org/10.3390/su141912269

**Publication and verification:** Published 27 September 2022. Metadata, abstract, relevant discussion, and stated limitations were verified through indexed original [MDPI article text](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/19/12269).

**Method and findings:** A LinkedIn-administered survey obtained responses from 221 supply chain professionals in Saudi Arabia. Partial least squares structural equation modelling identified positive relationships between digital transformation, supply chain procurement, and competitive advantage, with procurement mediating the transformation–advantage relationship.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports the role of procurement in connecting stakeholder information and organizational processes. These are modelled relationships in survey data, not experimental proof that software creates competitive advantage. The study spans industries and one national economy; transfer to Philippine construction requires contextual assessment.

## 3. Sivasubramaniam et al. (2024)

**APA 7:** Sivasubramaniam, N., Abeynayake, D. N., Shanika, V. G., & Lingasabesan, V. (2024). Insights into centralized procurement for construction material supply chain: A study in Sri Lanka. *FARU Journal, 11*(1), 47–56. https://doi.org/10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313

**Publication and verification:** Published 18 October 2024. Date, authors, pages, and abstract were verified on the [publisher platform](https://reference-global.com/article/10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313); the abstract is corroborated by the indexed [original journal PDF](https://faruj.sljol.info/articles/313/files/67120dd2b4394.pdf).

**Method and findings:** Ten semi-structured expert interviews were analyzed using manual content analysis. Participants identified potential cost reduction, improved procurement monitoring, and informed decisions, alongside risks involving competition, client satisfaction, innovation, and stakeholder confidence. Recommended responses include uniform procedures and clear supplier-selection, bidding, and contract-administration rules.

**Relevance and limit:** Directly supports examining centralization of construction material procurement while retaining explicit procedures and stakeholder participation. These are qualitative findings, not measured savings or a tested software intervention. Full results tables were not independently audited.

## 4. Gurgun et al. (2024)

**APA 7:** Gurgun, A. P., Kunkcu, H., Koc, K., Arditi, D., & Atabay, S. (2024). Challenges in the integration of e-procurement procedures into construction supply chains. *Buildings, 14*(3), Article 605. https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14030605

**Publication and verification:** Published 24 February 2024. Methods and findings were verified through indexed original [MDPI article](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/3/605) and [XML text](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/3/605/xml).

**Method and findings:** Literature and focus-group work identified 28 barriers. Section 3.3 specifies 198 questionnaire invitations, 162 returns, and 131 valid responses after exclusions. Responses were ranked using fuzzy TOPSIS; 15 semi-structured interviews provided further context. Unexpected cancellations, material-price variation, and industry uncertainty requiring changes emerged as leading barriers.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports procurement records that can accommodate changing orders and prices, rather than assuming stable transactions. Findings reflect Turkish practitioners' perceptions and rankings, not measured performance gains following implementation. Use 131 valid responses, rather than the less precise abstract wording of 131 practitioners contacted.

## 5. Parisi and Donyavi (2026)

**APA 7:** Parisi, L., & Donyavi, S. (2026). Advancing materials procurement processes in construction: A framework for optimising technology integration in SMEs' construction supply chains. *International Journal of Construction Management, 26*(7), 1365–1389. https://doi.org/10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610

**Publication and verification:** First published online 5 September 2025; the final volume/issue is dated 2026. Use 2026 consistently when citing the final reference. Metadata and abstract were verified in indexed original [Taylor & Francis PDF text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610).

**Method and findings:** The abstract reports mixed methods, including cases, interviews, surveys, and observations across six construction SMEs. A framework combining accessible digital tools reportedly reduced order-processing time by 34%; it also reports reduced waste and improved inventory accuracy. Phased implementation and feedback mechanisms supported adoption.

**Relevance and limit:** Direct evidence for integrated digital material procurement in smaller construction firms. Attribute the percentage to the studied framework and SMEs. It is a reported operational outcome, not a survey agreement score, but underlying measurements, comparators, and calculations were not independently audited; it is not a target or prediction for ConstructSync.

## 6. Bacarisas et al. (2026)

**APA 7:** Bacarisas, M. O., Lucos, J. M. M., Candelasa, S. M., Guzon, A., de Fátima Marcos, E., Apar-Aberia, M., Malang, B. P., & Malang, J. D. (2026). Bridging procurement gaps through digital tools: Evidence from selected Philippine national government agencies. *International Journal of Multidisciplinary: Applied Business and Education Research, 7*(1), 242–260. https://doi.org/10.11594/ijmaber.07.01.21

**Publication and verification:** Published 23 January 2026. Citation metadata and abstract were verified on the [original publisher page](https://ijmaberjournal.org/index.php/ijmaber/article/view/3137), rather than relying only on the Philippine E-Journals listing.

**Method and findings:** The abstract describes mixed-method research involving 60 procurement practitioners in NCR. Respondents perceived efficiency/timeliness improvements (average weighted mean 4.28) and transparency/compliance improvements (4.30). Adoption and operational efficiency were associated (r = .606, p < .001). Persistent barriers included poor system integration, limited skills, unstable connectivity, and resistance.

**Relevance and limit:** Offers Philippine evidence that digital procurement requires integration and organizational support. The means express perceptions; the correlation is not causal evidence or a measured cycle-time reduction. Full methods and tables were inaccessible, so the mixed-method characterization remains abstract-based.

## Access limitations and synthesis boundary

Direct MDPI page requests sometimes returned HTTP 429; indexed publisher text supplied the verified methods and findings identified above. Sivasubramaniam et al. was verified at abstract level through the publisher and indexed original PDF. Parisi and Donyavi's direct PDF request returned HTTP 403, and Bacarisas et al.'s publisher PDF could not be retrieved. For these three studies, the available original abstracts verify what the authors report, but do not establish that underlying measurements or analysis have been independently checked. Bibliographic existence and verified reporting should not be mistaken for a complete methodological quality audit.

The connection to ConstructSync is an application of the literature: shared records can connect material requests, approvals, supplier canvassing, purchase orders, budget checks, delivery, and inventory updates. Present this as the rationale for the proposed system and its later evaluation, not as a finding already established at HML. Any research gap should be limited to the contexts and capabilities addressed by these selected studies; this search does not establish that no comparable system or study exists elsewhere.
