## Primary-source records

### Chan and Owusu (2022)

**Primary article:** [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/12/2/198). DOI: [10.3390/buildings12020198](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings12020198).

The structured review examined 64 manuscripts using bibliometric, scientometric, thematic, and content analyses (§§3–4). Section 2 supports the procurement stages used in the introduction. Section 5 explicitly characterizes the framework as theoretical and calls for empirical, case-specific assessment.

The comparison is with linked procurement stages, not the paper's full discussion of intelligent procurement. Do not classify the proposed application as autonomous or attribute cost/time savings to this review.

### Alabdali and Salam (2022)

**Primary article:** [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/19/12269). DOI: [10.3390/su141912269](https://doi.org/10.3390/su141912269).

The survey obtained 221 responses; Table 1 identifies 214 Saudi respondents and seven others. “Predominantly based in Saudi Arabia” is more precise than describing every participant as Saudi. Appendix A identifies supplier selection, gathering procurement requirements, purchasing histories, procurement databases, and communication through information systems. PLS-SEM results support modeled relationships and procurement's mediation role (§§5.3–5.4).

Section 6.2 limits generalization across settings and notes that implementation challenges were not examined. Functional counterparts in the present study do not demonstrate the same organizational relationships or competitive advantage. The manuscript accordingly uses the survey to identify relevant activities rather than to predict benefits.

### Sivasubramaniam et al. (2024)

**Primary article:** [Original journal page](https://faruj.sljol.info/articles/10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313); [original journal PDF](https://faruj.sljol.info/articles/313/files/67120dd2b4394.pdf). DOI: [10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313](https://doi.org/10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313).

Ten purposively selected experts participated in semi-structured interviews, analyzed through manual content analysis (§3, p. 50). Section 4 frames the results as potential impacts because centralized procurement was not widespread in the setting. Monitoring multiple projects is supported by §4.1; difficulty satisfying individual project requirements appears in §4.2 (p. 51; Figure 3, p. 53).

The present study centralizes access to related information while preserving assigned review and sourcing responsibilities. The source does not establish that these arrangements are identical to centralized buying, nor does it measure savings from a tested software intervention. Relevant full-text sections and the results figure were checked; the earlier abstract-only access description is superseded.

### Gurgun et al. (2024)

**Primary article:** [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/3/605); [publisher XML](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/3/605/xml). DOI: [10.3390/buildings14030605](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14030605).

Literature and focus-group work identified 28 barriers. Section 3.3 reports 198 invitations, 162 returns, and 131 valid questionnaires; 15 interviews supplied additional context. Use 131 valid responses, rather than 131 invitations. Table 2 identifies internal integration before external integration. Sections 4–5 identify unexpected cancellations, price variation, and uncertainty requiring changes among leading barriers.

The rankings concern practitioners' judgments; §7 notes contextual limits and relatively few respondents with e-procurement experience. Connected records are a proposed response to changing information. The paper does not demonstrate that the present study's mixed sourcing prevents disruptions or stabilizes prices.

### Parisi and Donyavi (2026)

**Primary text:** [Taylor & Francis indexed publisher PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610). DOI: [10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610](https://doi.org/10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610).

The final PDF identifies 2026, volume 26, issue 7, pp. 1365–1389. Online-first publication was 5 September 2025. Methodology and Tables 1–3 describe six SMEs, 12 interviews, observations, and 60 survey responses: 30 before and 30 after implementation. This does not necessarily mean 60 distinct participants.

The reported 34% average order-processing reduction concerns the combined framework, which uses mobile tools, RFID, and cloud systems; Table 4 describes processing-time reductions of 30–40%. Conclusions and Limitations acknowledge a limited regional sample, some self-report bias, and no control group or alternative-framework comparison. The RRL reports the outcome without promising an equivalent improvement or attributing it to cloud records alone.

Direct PDF access can return HTTP 403; relevant indexed primary full-text sections were verified. This extends the earlier abstract-only verification but is not an independent reanalysis of raw data.

### Magdaraog (2023)

**Primary article:** [Publisher page](https://ijeber.com/digitalization-of-the-procurement-process-in-the-department-of-public-works-and-highways-in-the-province-of-masbate/); [full publisher PDF](https://ijeber.com/uploads2023/ijeber_03_109.pdf).

The publisher dates the article June 2023, volume 3, issue 3, pp. 180–230. The descriptive-inferential survey purposively selected 55 respondents across three DPWH district offices (pp. 204–206). The prose and Table A conflict over the employee/contractor breakdown (p. 205); retain only the consistent total of 55.

Conclusions and recommendations identify budget review, prerequisite checklists, post-award evidence, and secure-document-location concerns (pp. 223–225). The output is a proposed e-procurement process, not a deployed application evaluated for effectiveness. Favorable practice ratings do not establish full digitization or objective cycle-time reduction. Government tendering and public monitoring also differ from the present study's staff-mediated private purchasing. Historical legal background in the paper is not presented as current legal guidance.

### Tabangay and Siddayao (2024)

**Primary article:** [Publisher page](https://journal.aide-inc.net/index.php/aide-irj/article/view/115); [full publisher PDF](https://journal.aide-inc.net/index.php/aide-irj/article/download/115/101/104). DOI: [10.56648/aide-irj.v8i1.115](https://doi.org/10.56648/aide-irj.v8i1.115).

The article reports Waterfall development (pp. 131–132) and deployment to campus offices followed by an employee questionnaire using ISO 25010-based criteria and a five-point scale (p. 134). Phone and individual interviews also informed challenges and recommendations. Evaluation sample size is not reported.

“The Main Form” lists purchase request, PR approval, quotation, abstract of quotation, purchase order, budget affirmation, PO approval, and inspection and acceptance (pp. 137–138). An abstract of quotation is a summary of offers; the article does not establish automatic supplier selection. Table 1 and its discussion report 4.42 overall, 4.53 functional suitability, and 4.33 usability (pp. 138–139).

These are employee quality ratings, not independent ISO certification or before/after measurements of time or cost. Broad claims in the article about savings are not repeated as established findings. The RRL compares explicit functions and assessment orientation without claiming the campus system lacks transfers, external payment records, or unmentioned capabilities.

## Synthesis and verification boundaries

- The organizing concerns are connected procurement stages, coordination across projects, and implementation/assessment. Inventory, logistics, payment, and document functions appear only where they explain procurement relationships.
- Specific similarities identify corresponding functions or assessment approaches. Contextual differences and methodological limitations explain the boundaries of applying each study.
- The closing gap is limited to contextual implementation and assessment of the proposed workflow. The reviewed selection does not establish universal novelty or prove that earlier systems lack particular features.
- Shared information is distinct from centralized purchasing authority. A common record does not mean a common decision-maker or pooled bulk order.
- Review concepts, expert perceptions, modeled survey associations, employee ratings, and reported implementation outcomes retain their different evidential meanings.
- Functionality and user assessment is the agreed evaluation direction. No instrument, ISO edition, sample size, before/after operational study, or causal savings claim is invented for the present study.
- Publisher access restrictions sometimes require indexed primary text. A checked report is not an independently reproduced result.
- The final subsection contains eight connected paragraphs and seven APA 7 references. Its body is within the agreed 900–1,200-word range, excluding the heading and reference list.
