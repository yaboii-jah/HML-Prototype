# Diagram conventions for the ConstructSync thesis

Research date: **2026-10-13**. These foundational references explain modeling conventions. They are guidance for the thesis diagrams, **not additions to the recent Review of Related Literature list**. Published conventions are distinguished below from choices inferred for ConstructSync.

## ERD abstraction and exclusive Delivery sources

**Published guidance.** Oracle distinguishes a logical model of entities, attributes, identifiers, and relationships from a relational model of SQL tables, columns, and joins. Subject-area subviews can show selected parts of a model. This supports separating a readable business-data overview from a complete schema specification. Oracle also defines an arc as an exclusive group of relationships: only one can apply to an entity occurrence; making the arc mandatory requires one of those relationships. See [Oracle Data Modeler concepts, logical/relational models and arcs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/sql-developer-data-modeler/21.2/dmdug/data-modeler-concepts-usage.html), sections 1.3.4–1.3.5.

**ConstructSync inference.** Use the overview ERD to communicate business entities, meaningful identifiers, and cardinalities. Keep implementation columns, indexes, authorization policies, and exhaustive audit details in the schema/data dictionary. A generic Delivery can have exactly one source: a Purchase Order **or** a Stock Transfer. Show a mandatory exclusive arc or an explicit “exactly one source” constraint; two unrelated optional relationships alone would also permit both sources or neither. This logical choice does not prescribe Oracle as the database or require a separate Transfer Delivery entity. Source-side multiplicities still need to reflect the app's actual rules.

## Logical DFD boundaries, mediation, and balancing

**Published guidance.** A logical DFD explains information needed for business functions without implementation details. Processes transform information; stores retain it. DFDs do not show decision-path selection, timing, or repetition. External means outside the chosen system boundary, so company employees can be external to an application. Data passes between stores and external entities through processes. See [IBM on logical versus physical DFDs](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-flow-diagram), [University of Cape Town on DFD elements](https://www.cs.uct.ac.za/mit_notes/software/htmls/ch06s05.html), and [Kenneth A. Kozar, University of Colorado, on data versus control flow](https://spot.colorado.edu/~kozar/DFD.html).

The context diagram represents the whole system as one process, with external entities and boundary exchanges; internal processes and stores remain hidden. Level 1 decomposes the main functions and exposes logical stores. Every context input/output must remain represented at Level 1; a new boundary exchange requires updating both diagrams. Level 2 expands a selected Level 1 process while preserving that parent's boundary flows. See UCT's [context diagrams](https://www.cs.uct.ac.za/mit_notes/software/htmls/ch06s06.html), [Level 1 guidance](https://www.cs.uct.ac.za/mit_notes/software/htmls/ch06s07.html), [balancing](https://www.cs.uct.ac.za/mit_notes/software/htmls/ch06s10.html), and [Level 2 decomposition](https://www.cs.uct.ac.za/mit_notes/software/htmls/ch06s08.html).

**ConstructSync inference.** Declare whether the boundary covers direct application interactions or logical business-information exchanges. The current logical view retains Supplier as the quotation originator and issued-PO recipient, with a note that Procurement mediates these exchanges and suppliers do not directly access the application. Procurement's own flows represent review and sourcing/PO decisions rather than duplicate supplier documents. Apply this interpretation consistently at both levels. Include AI extraction and document storage services where their exchanges cross that boundary.

Group stores by coherent business information, rather than creating one store per database table. Keep Level 1 at major transformations, not screens or individual API actions. Use a targeted Level 2 only when a complex function needs explanation. For stock transfer, preserve source-site release authorization, logistics scheduling/dispatch, destination receipt/stock posting, and Accounting document review; do not add purchase-order or payment requirements to that route.

## Ordinary flowcharts, subprocesses, and independent branches

**Published guidance.** A basic flowchart shows procedural steps and decisions linked by directional arrows. Common symbols include terminators, activity boxes, decision diamonds, subprocess boxes, and on-page/off-page references. Subprocesses and references can move detailed work to another diagram or page. Swimlanes identify responsible people or departments. Microsoft distinguishes general-purpose flowcharts from formally specified methodologies. See [Microsoft's basic flowchart conventions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/visio/create-a-basic-flowchart-in-visio) and [process diagram types](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/visio/process-diagrams-in-visio).

BPMN gives gateways explicit execution semantics. Its parallel gateway creates parallel paths and synchronizes required incoming paths; it uses a plus-marked diamond. An ordinary flowchart connector does not acquire these semantics merely because paths meet. See [OMG BPMN 2.0.2](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0.2/PDF), sections 10.6.4 and 13.4.1.

**ConstructSync inference.** Show the main business path and decisions that materially change authorization or the route. Group routine validation, technical retries, and minor exceptions within subprocess descriptions. Label optional bypasses and meaningful business rework loops. Give cross-page references matching identifiers and destinations. Distinguish merging alternative routes from waiting for independent work: payment and fulfillment should synchronize only when the agreed business rule requires it. If black split/merge dots remain, define their meaning explicitly in the legend; do not imply an undocumented BPMN AND-join or add a payment dependency to stock transfers.
