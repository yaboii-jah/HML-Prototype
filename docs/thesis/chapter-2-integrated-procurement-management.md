# Integrated Procurement Management

Integrated procurement management connects material requirements, approvals, supplier evaluation, purchasing documents, and related information exchanges within a coordinated process. In construction operations, these activities involve several departments and project sites, making consistent records and clear responsibilities essential. Integration allows procurement information to remain connected as requests progress from site preparation to review, canvassing, budget verification, and purchase order generation. It also provides a basis for tracing the documents and decisions associated with each transaction.

[Chan and Owusu (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings12020198) reviewed 64 publications on electronic procurement and described digital sourcing, tendering, information exchange, and purchasing transactions. Their review identified organizational policies, technological readiness, and change management as adoption considerations. Supporting this organizational perspective, [Alabdali and Salam (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/su141912269) surveyed 221 supply-chain professionals and found positive relationships among digital transformation, procurement, and competitive advantage. Procurement also mediated the relationship between digital transformation and competitive advantage. Together, these studies support treating digital procurement as a combination of connected purchasing activities and organizational capabilities. Their findings provide a foundation for system development, while the performance of a particular application requires evaluation in its intended setting.

In construction, [Sivasubramaniam et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.4038/faruj.v11i1.313) examined centralized procurement in Sri Lanka through ten expert interviews. Participants identified potential benefits involving cost management, procurement monitoring, and informed purchasing decisions. They also described risks, including restricted competition, reduced stakeholder confidence, and difficulties meeting project-specific requirements. The authors recommended standardized procedures and explicit supplier-selection and contract-administration criteria. These qualitative findings suggest that centralized procurement should preserve individual project requirements while establishing common procedures. For multi-site operations, this supports maintaining clear project references, material specifications, and purchasing responsibilities within shared procurement records.

The importance of accommodating changing project conditions was reinforced by [Gurgun et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14030605). Their investigation examined 28 barriers to electronic procurement integration through practitioner questionnaires, barrier-ranking analysis, and 15 interviews. Leading barriers included unexpected order cancellations, material-price variations, and changing construction requirements. These findings highlight the need for procurement procedures that can respond to revisions during purchasing. Applied to ConstructSync, they support keeping requests, quotations, purchasing decisions, and supporting documents connected so authorized personnel can review discrepancies and reassess transactions when project conditions change.

Evidence of technology-supported procurement outcomes was provided by [Parisi and Donyavi (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/15623599.2025.2553610), who evaluated a procurement framework across six construction small and medium-sized enterprises. Their mixed-methods study incorporated mobile applications, RFID, and cloud-based systems, supported by phased implementation and feedback mechanisms. The authors reported a 34% reduction in order-processing time, together with improvements in material waste and inventory accuracy. These reported outcomes support the potential value of connecting procurement information through accessible digital tools. However, they reflect the framework's combined technologies and implementation conditions; ConstructSync would require its own evaluation to establish outcomes at HML Technologies and Services Inc.

A Philippine perspective was presented by [Bacarisas et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.11594/ijmaber.07.01.21), who examined digital procurement among 60 practitioners from selected national government agencies in the National Capital Region. Respondents reported favorable perceptions of procurement timeliness, transparency, and compliance. Digital-tool adoption was positively associated with operational efficiency, although poor system integration, limited technical skills, unstable connectivity, and resistance to change remained concerns. These survey findings provide local implementation context, with their government setting acknowledged. They reinforce the importance of coordinated processes, accessible records, and user support when implementing procurement systems.

The reviewed literature emphasizes the importance of connected records, standardized procedures, and coordinated responsibilities in integrated procurement management. Clear workflows, reliable information exchange, and adequate user support help organizations address changing construction requirements and challenges in implementing digital procurement processes.
