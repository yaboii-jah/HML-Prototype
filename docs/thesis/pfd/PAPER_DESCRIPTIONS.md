# Process Flowchart Descriptions

## 1. Request and Fulfillment Overview

The figure presents the main sequence for fulfilling a Material Request Form (MRF) within an existing project. Site Personnel submit material requirements to the Project Manager, who determines whether the request should be approved, returned for correction, or rejected. Following approval, Procurement checks available stock and allocates the requested quantities to supplier purchasing, transfers from other project sites, or a combination of both. A single request may therefore include materials obtained through different sourcing routes. Cleared supplier deliveries and dispatched transfers proceed to receiving. Accepted quantities are subsequently recorded in site inventory, while supporting receipt evidence proceeds independently to documentation and review.

## 2. Supplier Purchasing and PO Approval

The figure illustrates the purchasing process for materials allocated to supplier procurement. Procurement evaluates supplier quotations, selects suppliers for the required materials, and prepares and submits purchase orders (POs). Accounting reviews the proposed costs, applicable taxes and adjustments, budget availability, and payment terms. The review may remain on hold, return the PO for correction, result in rejection, or proceed to approval. Returned POs undergo correction and further review, while rejected POs end the corresponding purchasing route. Once approved, the agreed terms are recorded and the PO is archived. Procurement then issues the approved PO to the supplier, allowing the transaction to proceed to payment coordination.

## 3. Payment Coordination and Delivery Clearance

The figure distinguishes the payment and delivery-clearance procedures for upfront and credit arrangements after a supplier PO has been issued. Finance coordinates payments processed outside the application, while Accounting archives supporting payment evidence and verifies the amounts paid against the net balance. For upfront arrangements, full verified net payment is required before Accounting grants delivery clearance. Incomplete payment or unresolved evidence returns for further coordination and verification. Under credit terms, Accounting may clear delivery while Finance continues to coordinate payment independently. Partial payments reduce the outstanding balance, and unsettled amounts remain subject to follow-up until verified settlement is achieved. Delivery clearance and balance settlement remain separate milestones.

## 4. Inter-site Stock Transfer

The figure outlines the transfer process for materials sourced from another project site. Procurement confirms that the available materials are suitable for the request and obtains review from destination Site Personnel when required. Availability is rechecked before a transfer is proposed to the source site. Source Site Personnel may approve the full quantity, approve only part of it, or reject the release. Approved quantities proceed to Logistics for scheduling and dispatch, while rejected or unreleased quantities return to material sourcing. Dispatch deducts the dispatched quantities from source stock before destination receiving. This route fulfills material requirements without supplier quotation, PO preparation, or payment processing.

## 5. Delivery, Receiving, and Inventory

The figure presents the delivery, receiving, and inventory activities that begin after supplier delivery clearance or site-transfer dispatch. Logistics schedules supplier deliveries either directly to the requesting site or through the warehouse. For warehouse deliveries, Procurement records receipt before Logistics forwards the materials to the designated project site. Dispatched site transfers enter directly at destination receiving. Site Personnel record accepted quantities and any shortages, damage, or other discrepancies, then post the accepted materials to site inventory. Warehouse receipt alone does not increase site stock. Receipt evidence proceeds independently to document review. Missing transfer quantities return to material sourcing without reversing the source stock deduction, while supplier discrepancies remain associated with the relevant PO and delivery records.

## 6. Receipt Documentation and Review

The figure illustrates the documentation and review process initiated when warehouse or site receipt evidence becomes available. Receiving personnel archive the generated delivery receipts and the current signed receipts associated with each received batch. Accounting reviews the applicable supporting documents to determine whether the evidence is complete and acceptable. Missing, incomplete, or unacceptable documents return to the receiving personnel for correction, completion, and re-archiving before another review. Once the evidence meets the applicable requirements, document review is recorded as completed. This process operates independently of inventory posting and payment settlement. Internal transfers are reviewed using receipt evidence and do not require supplier invoices or payment proof.
